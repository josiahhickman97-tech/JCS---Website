Real-world challenges

Industrial problems that demand outdoor design

Different physics than a store or school: distance, weather, vehicles, and shift work.

Challenge 01

Materials and equipment walk off

Copper, tools, trailers, and staged materials sit in yards that nobody can watch all shift.

What we design: Yard cameras with the right lenses, heights, and recording so you can identify activity and vehicles - not just see a blob.

Challenge 02

Docks and gates are chaos points

Trucks stack up, seals break, and unauthorized vehicles slip into active zones.

What we design: Dock and gate coverage, optional LPR, and access control on pedestrian and vehicle gates where it fits operations.

Challenge 03

Indoor kits die outdoors

Consumer-grade cameras and poor enclosures fail in heat, rain, dust, and vibration.

What we design: Outdoor-rated hardware, proper mounts, and pathways planned for the environment - not leftover retail stock.

Challenge 04

Distance kills weak cabling

Cameras at the far fence line drop offline when someone daisy-chained copper past its limit.

What we design: Fiber and structured runs designed for site scale, with room to add cameras as the yard grows.

FAQ

Industrial security questions

Can you cover large outdoor yards effectively?

Yes - with the right camera types, mounting heights, and backbone cabling. Indoor dome kits pointed at a fence line are not a yard solution.

Do you offer license plate recognition?

LPR is available where gate or lot workflows need vehicle identity. We recommend it when it solves a real operational problem, not as a default upsell.

How do you handle multi-shift sites?

We schedule disruptive work around production when possible and train more than one supervisor so nights and weekends are not blind.

What about expanding as the site grows?

We plan spare capacity in pathways and recording so new buildings or yard sections can add cameras without starting over.
