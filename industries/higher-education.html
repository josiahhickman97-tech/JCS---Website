Real-world challenges

Higher-ed challenges that store kits miss

Scale, openness, and many stakeholders - solved with phased design, not one giant box of cameras.

Challenge 01

Sprawl across many buildings

Academic halls, housing, athletics, and remote lots sit on different networks and budgets. A single-site retail design falls apart.

What we design: Phased campus plans with standardized platforms, building-by-building prioritization, and cabling that connects the footprint.

Challenge 02

Residence halls need different rules

Housing wants convenience for residents and restriction for non-residents. Keys and shared fobs create audit nightmares.

What we design: Building and wing-level access with credentials that can be changed when residents turn over each term.

Challenge 03

Parking and pathway incidents

Lots and walkways generate safety concerns and vehicle-related incidents that outdoor cameras alone cannot always identify.

What we design: Exterior coverage with optional license plate recognition where policy and need support it.

Challenge 04

Labs and research spaces

Restricted labs, equipment rooms, and after-hours research create access and documentation requirements beyond classroom doors.

What we design: Segmented access zones and video on critical corridors so safety and research leadership share a clear trail.

How we install here

Phased installs that respect the academic calendar

We work around move-in, finals, commencements, and blackout research periods. Big campuses rarely go live in one weekend - and should not try to.

  • Priority buildings first (housing, public safety, high-risk labs)

  • Documentation packages campus safety can hand to new staff

FAQ

Campus security questions

Can you phase a multi-year campus plan?

Yes. Many colleges start with highest-risk buildings and parking, then expand as budgets and renovations allow - on a consistent platform.

Do you work with campus IT?

Always. Camera and access systems live on the network. We plan addressing, bandwidth, and storage with the people who own infrastructure.

Is LPR required for every campus?

No. LPR is optional where parking enforcement or investigation needs justify it. We recommend it only when it solves a real campus problem.

How do you handle residence hall turnover?

Access systems should make credential changes routine. We design for semester turnover, not permanent metal keys that never get returned.
