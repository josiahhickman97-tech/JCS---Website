Real-world challenges

What bad cabling costs you later

Cabling problems show up as 'camera issues' months after a cheap install.

Challenge 01

Cameras drop at the far end

Copper pushed past distance limits or poor terminations cause intermittent devices that waste service calls.

What we design: Correct media choice (Cat6 vs fiber), tested terminations, and runs within design limits.

Challenge 02

Wireless as a permanent plan

Wi-Fi cameras and temporary links become the default and then fail in weather, interference, or dense walls.

What we design: Prefer structured wired paths for fixed security devices; use wireless only where it is the right tool.

Challenge 03

No path for the next project

Today's install fills every conduit and leaves no spare for next year's cameras or doors.

What we design: Pathways and spare capacity planned with growth in mind - especially on industrial and campus sites.

Challenge 04

Messy undocumented work

Future techs cannot find IDs, patch fields, or which drop feeds which camera.

What we design: Labeled, documented structured cabling with clean terminations and as-builts when the job needs them.

FAQ

Network cabling FAQs

Do you only cable for security systems?

Security is our focus, but structured cabling often supports APs, phones, and business drops in the same design when it makes sense.

When do I need fiber instead of copper?

Long distances, building-to-building links, outdoor spans, and high-bandwidth backbones are common fiber use cases. We specify based on length and load.

Can you fix a messy existing closet?

Often yes - cleanup, re-termination, and documentation can stabilize devices without a full rebuild.

Will cabling support PoE cameras?

We design for power and distance requirements of modern PoE cameras and related devices.

How do you minimize downtime?

Phased pulls, after-hours work when needed, and clear cutover plans for active networks.
