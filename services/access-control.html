Real-world challenges

Why keys and ad-hoc unlocks fail

Access control is not a gadget - it is accountability for the doors that matter.

Challenge 01

Keys multiply and never come back

Staff turnover, contractors, and spare keys leave you unsure who can still walk into the building.

What we design: Individual credentials with fast revoke and clear permission levels by door or zone.

Challenge 02

Doors stay unlocked for convenience

Someone props a back door or unlocks early for deliveries - and security policy dies for the rest of the day.

What we design: Schedules and door status that match operations, with video or contacts when doors stay open past policy.

Challenge 03

Restricted rooms without a paper trail

Cash rooms, medicine storage, evidence, IT closets, and labs get shared keys and informal access.

What we design: Limited credentials and time-stamped logs for the doors that create real liability.

Challenge 04

Multi-building chaos

Schools, campuses, and multi-site operators need different rules per building without five different systems.

What we design: Scalable access design with consistent management for staff who run more than one door set.

FAQ

Access control FAQs

Cloud or on-premise access control?

Both are options. Cloud suits multi-site remote management; on-premise fits certain IT and policy needs. We recommend based on doors, users, and who will administer the system.

Can we keep some existing door hardware?

Often yes. We assess locks, frames, and power before specifying what to reuse versus replace.

How fast can we remove a former employee?

With electronic credentials, revoke is typically immediate in the software - far faster and cleaner than collecting metal keys.

Does access integrate with cameras?

Yes. Pairing access events with nearby cameras is one of the highest-value combinations for investigations and daily operations.

Do you train our staff?

Yes. If administrators cannot manage credentials confidently, the system fails. Training is part of go-live.
