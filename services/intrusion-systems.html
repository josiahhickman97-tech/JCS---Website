Real-world challenges

Why intrusion projects fail without design

A panel full of sensors is worthless if zones are wrong or everyone ignores the alerts.

Challenge 01

Cameras without a wake-up call

Incidents finish before anyone opens a live view. Recording alone is not a response plan.

What we design: Sensors and zones that notify the right people immediately when something opens or moves after hours.

Challenge 02

False alarms train people to ignore them

Poor placement and sloppy zones create noise until nobody trusts the system.

What we design: Careful zone design, device selection, and tuning for the environment and schedule.

Challenge 03

No link to video

An alarm goes off and responders have no quick way to see what triggered it.

What we design: Intrusion designed alongside cameras so verification is fast when both systems are in place.

Challenge 04

One-size zones for a complex building

Warehouses, schools, and multi-tenant sites need different arming and partition logic.

What we design: Zoning and schedules matched to how areas are used - not a single all-or-nothing arming model if the site needs more.

FAQ

Intrusion system FAQs

Do I still need cameras if I have an alarm?

They solve different problems. Intrusion notifies; video shows what happened. Most sites that care about response want both.

Can you reduce false alarms on an existing system?

Often yes - through device placement, zone changes, schedules, and replacing problem sensors.

Do you monitor alarms 24/7?

We design and install systems and can discuss monitoring options based on your needs. Tell us how you want notifications handled during assessment.

Will intrusion work with our doors and cameras?

Yes - layered design with access and video is a core part of how we recommend systems.

What about warehouses and large open spaces?

Device choice and zone layout change for high ceilings, docks, and outdoor-adjacent spaces. We design for the building, not a house-style kit.
